When I resigned from my job, I was exhausted in a way that sleep couldn't fix.

It wasn't just tiredness. It was a deeper kind of empty, the kind that builds quietly over years until one day you realise you don't know what you enjoy anymore, what you want, or who you are outside of work. I had no clarity about my career, no enthusiasm for much of anything, and no real sense of what I wanted my life to look like.

Everyone around me had questions. Why did you resign without something else lined up? What is your plan? I didn't have good answers. I just knew I needed to stop before I could figure out where to go next.

So I went to New Zealand.

I didn't go with an agenda. I went to be somewhere completely different, to hike, to look at things that had nothing to do with a screen or a career or anyone's expectations. New Zealand made that easy. The landscape there is so rich and so overwhelming in its beauty that it becomes very difficult to think about anything else. Mountains. Hot springs. Ancient rocks shaped by tectonic forces over millions of years. Trails that ask you to be fully present just to stay on them.

I learned things I had never thought to be curious about before. How geothermal activity works, how tectonic plates shift and reshape the earth over time, how something as ordinary as a rock can hold an extraordinary amount of history. Curiosity came back to me quietly, in a place where I wasn't trying to be productive.

The disconnection was complete. No work emails. No LinkedIn. No sense of time structured around deliverables. For the first time in years, I was simply somewhere, doing something, with no outcome attached to it.

In Auckland, I attended a botanical drawing class. I sat in a room with strangers and drew a fanged pitcher plant for a few hours. I haven't done anything like that in a long time. Art had been part of my life once, and somewhere along the way I had let it go. That afternoon reminded me it was still there, waiting.

I am aware that I was fortunate. Not everyone can step away. Not everyone has the financial cushion or the circumstances that allow for a break like this. I don't take that lightly. But I think the broader truth applies at any scale. We underestimate, consistently and significantly, what rest does to us. Not just physical rest, but the kind of rest that comes from being somewhere unfamiliar, away from the identity you carry in your usual life.

I came back from New Zealand refreshed in a way I hadn't felt in years. Enthusiastic again. Clear about what I wanted to do next and why. The burnout hadn't just lifted, it had been replaced by something that felt like direction.

New Zealand will stay with me for the rest of my life. Not just as a place I visited, but as a reminder of what becomes possible when you finally give yourself permission to stop.
